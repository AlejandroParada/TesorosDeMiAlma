# 18 The Gift of Fixing the Soul

"Oh Carpenter of Nazareth" ("Broken Things Can Be Repaired," April 2006)

My father could fix the usual electrical and electronic things of the 80s and 90s. It was common that he would always have some radio or TV to repair (especially the ones at home). More than anything, he didn't know electronics—it was pure talent for repairing. He knew how to check and what to test, quickly figuring out what the problem was. I was there to hand him the tools he needed and be an apprentice with great curiosity. One day he told me, "Don't touch me—I have electricity!" and I was barefoot and didn't understand or know what electricity was. Well, that day I learned what electricity was! He was insulated—he knew the elementary physics laws of electricity—and I "grounded" him and felt a strong electrical discharge through my body when I got close to see and touched his arm. I think God's mercy was very great that day for us not to get "stuck" to the current that day.

But what I learned most was seeing people's faces light up when they received their repaired appliance, and even more when they asked him how much it cost. "It's nothing," he would respond, "for you, you need it, and I know your family does too"—mainly they were elderly people with limited resources. I could see that my father didn't just fix appliances but also fixed the souls of those who came.

In Nazareth, the narrow road
that takes your breath and tires your feet,
passes where once lived
the Carpenter of Nazareth.

Along the dusty road
the townspeople used to come
and on the Carpenter's bench
deposit what was broken.

A little girl brought a broken doll,
a man brought a plow, a woman a chair.
"Carpenter, can you fix it?"
the three asked hopefully.

Each one receives what they requested:
the doll, the chair, or the plow;
the broken thing they had brought him
he returns perfectly repaired.

Year after year up the steep hill,
with slow steps and melancholy,
travel souls loaded with sorrow and pain
who implore day after day:

"Oh, Carpenter of Nazareth,
will it be possible for you to repair
my broken heart,
and my truly broken life?"

And through His love and great goodness,
His sweet life will interweave
with our broken one
and create new life.

"Oh, Carpenter of Nazareth,
convert to full perfection
the broken idols of my being:
desire, hope, faith, and aspiration!"
