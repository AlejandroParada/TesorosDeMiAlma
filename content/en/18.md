# 18 The Gift of Mending the Soul

*"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."* - [Matthew 11:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/11?lang=eng&id=p28#p28)
*("Broken Things to Mend," General Conference, April 2006)*

My father had a special gift for repairing the electrical and electronic things typical of the 1980s and 1990s. It was common for him always to have some radio or television to fix, especially the ones in our home and those of the neighbors who asked him for help.

More than technical knowledge of electronics, it was pure natural talent for repairing. He knew instinctively how to check and what to test. He quickly figured out what the problem was, following a logic that seemed to come from within.

I was there to hand him the tools he needed and to be an apprentice full of curiosity. One day he warned me: "Don't touch me—I have electricity!" I was barefoot and did not understand or really know what electricity was.

Well, that day I found out, and I learned what electricity was in the most direct way possible. He was insulated because he knew the basic laws of electrical physics, but I "grounded" him when, out of curiosity, I came close to look and touched his arm. I felt a strong electrical shock run through my whole body.

I believe God's mercy was very great that day, so that we did not "stay stuck" to the current. It was a physics lesson I never forgot.

## The true gift

But what I learned most was not about electricity, but about the human heart. It was seeing people's faces light up when they received their appliance repaired, working like new.

And even more when they, with a certain worry, asked him: "How much do I owe you?"

"It's nothing," my father would answer simply. "For you it is nothing. You need it, and I know your family does too."

They were mainly elderly people of limited means, neighbors who trusted his skill and his kindness.

I could see that my father did not only fix appliances; he also mended the souls of those who came to him. He gave them back hope, dignity, and the peace of knowing that someone cared for them without expecting anything in return.

## The Carpenter of Nazareth

That experience led me to reflect on Him who is the true Repairer of broken souls. Inspired by the hymn "Ah, Carpenter of Nazareth," I share these verses, which take on new meaning:

---

*In Nazareth, the narrow road  
that takes the breath and tires the feet,  
passes by where once there lived  
the Carpenter of Nazareth.*

*Along the dusty road  
the people of the town would come  
and on the Carpenter's bench  
lay down what had been broken.*

*A broken doll the little girl would bring,  
a plow the man, a chair the woman.  
"Carpenter, can you repair it?"  
the three would ask, full of hope.*

*Each one receives what was requested:  
the doll, the chair, or the plow;  
the broken thing they had brought  
is returned perfectly repaired.*

---

*Year after year the steep hill,  
with slow step and melancholy,  
souls laden with sorrow and pain  
walk, pleading day after day:*

*"Ah, Carpenter of Nazareth,  
will it be possible for You to repair  
my shattered heart,  
and my life, truly broken?"*

*And by His love and great kindness,  
He will weave His sweet life  
with our broken one  
and create new life.*

*"Ah, Carpenter of Nazareth,  
turn into full perfection  
the broken idols of my being:  
desire, hope, faith, and aspiration!"*

---

## The eternal lesson

As my father lovingly repaired the broken appliances of those in need, Jesus Christ, the Carpenter of Nazareth, can repair our shattered lives, our broken hearts, our lost hopes.

He charges nothing for His service, because His love is free and infinite. We only need to come to Him in faith, laying on His divine bench everything in us that is broken.

And He, with skilled hands and a heart full of compassion, will return us perfectly repaired, renewed, and full of new life.

That is the greatest gift: the power to mend not only things, but souls.
