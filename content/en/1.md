# 1 I Learned to Ride a Bicycle

*"Behold, my Spirit is upon you... walk with me"* - [Moses 6:34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/6?lang=eng&id=p34#p34)

When I was little, the children on the block rode their bicycles along the sidewalk for safety, since the street was far too dangerous for us. I did not know how to ride a bicycle, and I did not have one either, so I only watched from a distance as the others had fun on their shiny bicycles.

One day, a neighbor who was the father of one of the children riding bicycles had come home from work and noticed my situation. He paid attention and asked me why I did not join the other children to play with them. I answered, ashamed: "It's just that I don't know how to ride a bicycle."

"Oh, but don't worry, I'll teach you," he said with a kind smile.

The next day, Sergio suggested: "We're going over to the Prado. It's only about four blocks from here. Come with us." My brother and I went with his family, taking the bicycles they had.

At the "Hotel del Prado," which in those days was a restaurant barely managing to stay open, there was an old outdoor dance floor they no longer used. It would be the perfect place to learn to ride a bicycle.

Sergio, this good neighbor, said to me: "Come on, hop on." He carefully adjusted the height of the seat and explained how to pedal and move the handlebars so the bicycle would "work." Then he leaned close to my ear and, as if whispering, said: "Alejandro, don't worry. You can do this, you can do this. You're going to make it!"

He started running alongside me, at first holding the handlebars firmly and pushing me from the bicycle seat. At one point he said, "Move your feet, come on, come on, come on," and I found the courage to pedal.

"Good, good, good!" he shouted with enthusiasm.

After a while he said: "You can control the bike. I'm letting go of the handlebars now." And sure enough, I could move the handlebars, and I really was controlling the bicycle.

"Come on, come on, come on! You can do this!" he kept encouraging me.

He was still running with me, pushing the seat. "Push, push, push!" And I could feel his breath and his intense encouragement, but I began to notice that his voice sounded farther and farther away. My brother, who was nearby watching, was astonished and shouted: "You already know how to ride a bicycle!"

And I answered: "WHAAAT???"

I looked to the side, and Sergio was no longer there. I was going fast, but what I still did not know was how to brake. He had never explained that to me! When I looked back and confirmed that Sergio was not running beside me, I stopped watching what was in front of me and crashed into a tree. Thankfully I was not going very fast, and I only got a few scrapes.

"Come on, come on, up you get, it was nothing!" Sergio said, with that encouragement so typical of him, when he came running over. "I was just about to explain how the brakes worked."

It turned out that bicycle was very easy to stop, because it had three brakes: the front one, the back one, and also a coaster brake (if you pedal backward, the bicycle stops). Well, I learned very well what happens if I do not brake, in a very practical and memorable way.

We rode our bicycles home after that beautiful experience, and after the great effort of this father of my friend next door.

Life has its turns, and a few years passed as we all grew up. One day I heard news that saddened me deeply: Sergio had suffered a stroke at work. What sad news, but he was able to pull through, and before long he was discharged and sent home.

He had lost the movement on the left side of his body. I learned that they had set up pulleys and ropes so he could do rehabilitation and physical therapy exercises, which he had to do every day. His children were studying and working, and they did not have time to help Sergio with the exercises. His wife cooked and, between the household chores, helped Sergio with the therapy.

One day I realized what was going on and said to myself: "This woman can't take any more. I have to do something." So I went up to her and said: "Can I help you with Sergio?"

Her face lit up. "Really?" she asked, hopeful.

"I have a little time before I have to be at the Liceo," I answered.

I went to see Sergio. There he was, as if "switched off," and he seemed to be "someone else." I greeted him, and I think he did not recognize me at first. People change with time.

"Children grow up. He's Teresa's son. Do you remember him?" his wife explained. His gaze was distant, and he hardly spoke.

I said to him: "Don't worry. I'm here to help you." I placed the hand and the arm he could not move into a harness, and with the other hand he had to pull hard to lift it. It was a simple exercise, but for his condition it was very difficult.

I began doing the exercises with him, one by one, encouraging him the whole time. At one moment I leaned close to his ear and, as if whispering, said exactly the same words he had said to me years before: "Sergio, don't worry. You can do this, you can do this. You're going to make it!"

In that moment his eyes filled with courage, as if a light had come into them, and then a tear welled up. He remembered that magical moment in the Prado, but this time he was the one receiving the gift he had once given me.

Sergio and I kept doing the exercises until I no longer had time to be with him, for I had to focus on my studies. He had recovered an important part of his mobility.

This experience taught me that when we give love from the heart, it comes back multiplied. Like the Spirit of the Lord who accompanies us when we serve others, Sergio had taught me more than how to ride a bicycle: he taught me the transforming power of encouragement, patience, and selfless love.
