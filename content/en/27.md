# What Did They Do to My Father?

> **"And now, he imparteth his word by angels unto men, yea, not only men but women also. Now this is not all; little children do have words given unto them many times, which confound the wise and the learned."** (Alma 32:23)

> **"Verily I say unto you, Except ye be converted, and become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven."** (Matthew 18:3)

## The old gentleman at sacrament meeting

When we were children, a very elderly man came to the chapel—someone we affectionately called "the old gentleman." We would see him arrive for sacrament meeting, which in those days was the last meeting on Sundays.

My mother told my brother and me to sit near him, because he could not hear well and struggled to follow the hymns. So when we sang, we sang closer to his ear so he could hear, and we pointed with a finger in the hymnbook to show him where the song was. I do not know for certain whether he could read every verse, but we did it anyway.

We sang hymns together such as *"Let Us Oft Speak Kind Words..."*. He would tell us—at least he told me—that he had grandchildren my age, and that being with us made him feel as if he were with them.

## The war at home over coming to Church

He told us he had great difficulty attending. He came when they let him. I thought he was no longer a child, and yet at home they treated him almost as if he were. He himself helped us understand that.

In his household they argued with him a great deal and "made war" on him for coming to the chapel. There were heated discussions: he wanted them to understand why he went to Church and why he asked them to let him go.

For a time we stopped seeing him. Later I learned everything that had happened.

## The Bishop's call

One day the Bishop called me to speak with him in the bishop's office. I thought: "Oh no… what have I done wrong?" Sometimes we got into mischief, and I assumed I had been found out.

The Bishop said: "What did you do, Alejandro? What did you do?" He left me thinking and searching inside myself for what I might have done. Then he said: "Sit there. Let me tell you. Do you remember the brother who used to come for a while and then stopped coming? Well… that brother has passed away."

The news hit me. But the Bishop added that something else had happened before that.

## A daughter's question

One day a woman came expressly to speak with the Bishop. She asked him with pain and wonder: "What did you do to my father? What happened to him?"

She explained that her father no longer argued with them as before. He had changed in his manner. He had become closer to his grandchildren in a way she had never known before. He spoke to them with gentleness and care.

"Please tell me what you did to my father to make him change," she insisted.

At first the Bishop did not quite know what to answer. He only knew that some children had helped him during the meeting when he came. The woman continued: "Tell me, Bishop, what do you people have that can change even the hardest and roughest person?"

She also said that before dying, her father had barely managed to say something she did not fully understand: "Two children… it was two children… it was them."

She asked the Bishop what that meant. He explained everything to her: the power of the Holy Ghost in people's hearts, and that many times words are given to little children… words that confound the wise and the learned.

## The peace she was seeking

Then she asked: "How can we find that peace my father had?"

The Bishop answered that the missionaries could explain more to her about that peace, and asked if she would receive them.

"Yes, please!" the woman replied.

## What I learned as a child

Years later I understand that experience better. We did nothing extraordinary in the eyes of the world: we simply sat nearby, sang a little louder, and pointed to the verses of a hymn. But the Lord can use small things to soften a heart hardened by conflict.

That brother found in the chapel not only hymns and meetings: he found tenderness, belonging, and the influence of the Holy Ghost. And that peace was so real that his own daughter recognized it, longed for it, and wanted to know where it came from.

Thus I learned that the Gospel of Jesus Christ transforms lives, sometimes through the simple gestures of children, and that the Lord prepares hearts—even in the midst of sorrow—to receive His message of peace.
