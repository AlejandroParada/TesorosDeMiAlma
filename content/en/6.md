# 6 The Story of the Home I Visited as a Young Man

"...he said to the disciple: Behold your mother..." (John 19:27)

I remember as a young man being assigned to visit an elderly sister along with my companion in what was then called "Home Teaching." The sister's name was Angélica, and I believe there's no more appropriate name than that for her. By the way, this was my first assignment to visit with a companion, which filled me with anxiety and nerves—a whole mix of conflicting emotions.

The sister was very happy to know we would be visiting her. We introduced ourselves at the chapel on Sunday to coordinate a day to visit her, and we also asked for her address. She told us she was delighted that we would be her "home teachers," and said she would wait for us with her sisters at her house and that we should be punctual—it would be the following Wednesday at 4 in the afternoon.

The day of the visit arrived, and we went to the address she gave us, which was located on Millán Avenue. When we arrived at the address, we could see the house in the distance, set back because it had a large front yard with grass. At first we doubted that the address was written correctly, because when we arrived we saw that it was a nursing home. We looked carefully and they were already sitting outside because the day was very nice, and we saw several ladies sitting, including Sister Angélica among them. We approached and Sister Angélica introduced us to her sisters. They were very radiant and happy to see us. There was even an elderly lady, the oldest of all, whom she called "she's like my mother"—all quite lucid. I really don't remember seeing elderly women so happy and content, and Angélica was that connection that was part of their happiness!

Angélica told them, "This is how they are, look at them well because when we are on the other side they are the same, and you should listen to their message—they are priests of God." Then we shared a brief message and they were very pleased with our visit.

On another occasion, Angélica told us some things at the chapel. She said they had told her, "Our children and grandchildren don't come to visit us anymore." And Angélica told them, "Don't worry, from now on you are my sister, you have me now—I am your sister and I will love you as my sister." She said that to each one and told them not to worry because they wouldn't be alone since they were with their sister. To the eldest she said, "You will be my mother and I will be your daughter!" This simple act of love forever changed the lives of those women—they radiated light, love, and happiness because someone forgot about herself and gave love to others. Christ said, "I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you" (John 14:18) and said "Behold your mother" to his disciple to care for his mother.

I have seen acts of love, but none as special as this one—Angélica's act of someone who, having nothing but her strength, forgets about herself to give love and care to her neighbors. This is a treasure for my soul.
