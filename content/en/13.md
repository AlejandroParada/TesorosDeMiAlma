# 13 A Couple Lost at Tres Cruces

*“O Lord, whither shall I go to obtain food to feed my family, that they may not perish in the wilderness?”* — [1 Nephi 16:23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/16?lang=eng&id=p23#p23)

One ordinary day I was doing some routine shopping in downtown Montevideo. In that area stands the most important bus terminal in the country for connecting all of Uruguay, known as Tres Cruces. In that same complex there is a fairly large shopping mall.

I was walking toward that mall when, suddenly, I felt a very clear spiritual prompting: “Go to the mall.”

At first I did not give that impression much importance, but again I felt it, with greater intensity: “Go to the mall.”

I said to myself, “All right, I will go to the mall,” and I changed course toward the shopping center.

## The impressions grow stronger

When I reached the complex, I saw that there were escalators going up to the mall and others going down to the bus terminal. At that moment I felt another clear impression: “Go down.”

I obeyed and headed toward the terminal in that direction.

“Keep walking,” I felt again, with clarity.

So I continued walking through a terminal full of people coming and going with luggage, waiting for buses, saying goodbye to family. It was the typical bustle of a transportation terminal.

“Stop,” I suddenly felt.

I stopped at once and looked around, trying to understand what I was supposed to do or what I was supposed to see.

## The providential meeting

It was then that I saw a couple speaking in English. They looked confused and disoriented in the midst of all that crowd. I drew near out of curiosity and could see that they were wearing the name tags of missionaries of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

They were an **Elder and Sister, a missionary couple**, clearly lost in that immense terminal. They had just arrived in the country, they had received their first missionary assignment, and they had no idea where they were or what they needed to do to reach their destination!

In my imagination I could hear them when, with sincere faith, they prayed together: “Please help us, O Lord. We do not know how to get where we are supposed to go.”

## “I was sent to you”

When I reached the place where they stood, I felt a clear impression to say to them: “I was sent to you.”

They looked at me in surprise and asked, hopeful: “Do you speak English?”

“Yes, I do!” I answered them with a smile.

They looked at each other in astonishment and relief. The sister missionary, with tears beginning to form in her eyes, exclaimed: “The Lord is watching over us!”

“I’m a member of the Church,” I explained, to set them completely at ease.

The missionary **elder** answered in disbelief: “Really?”

“I’m going to help you,” I assured them at once.

## Concrete help

I helped them find the right bus that would take them to their first assignment in the beautiful coastal city of Maldonado, where they would begin their missionary service in Uruguay.

I explained how the transportation system worked, which ticket to buy, where to get off, and I even gave them a few basic orientations about Uruguayan culture that would be useful in their work.

While we waited for their bus, I could see in their faces the peace and gratitude they felt at knowing that the Lord had answered their prayer in so direct and loving a way.

## The personal lesson

I am deeply grateful that the same kind of spiritual impressions I felt when I was a missionary are still present and active in my life today. These whisperings of the Holy Ghost continue to come through constant study of the scriptures, sincere prayer, and daily meditation.

This experience confirmed to me once again that when we keep our hearts open and willing to serve, the Lord can use us as instruments to answer the prayers of His other children.

The whisperings of the Holy Ghost truly are the purest source of joy in my life, and experiences like this remind me that God is constantly orchestrating miraculous meetings to bless His children in moments of need.

Sometimes we are the answer to someone else’s prayer, and at other times someone else is the answer to ours. But always, always, the Lord is aware of our needs.
