20 Your Mom Is Very Good (She gave the saved bread to a poor family waiting outside with their horse-drawn cart)

"...inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me" (Matthew 25:40)

There was a time each week when, without fail, a horse-drawn cart would arrive at the gate of the hallway of my grandparents' apartment in the Prado neighborhood of Montevideo. Sometimes it was full of garbage bags, other times with cardboard, other times with bottles that the family aboard the cart collected from houses and garbage cans they found along their way. On one occasion they stopped at the hallway door, and I, who was a little kid maybe around 8 years old, saw them from afar and told my mother that they were knocking at the gate. She called me, I went inside and she gave me a large bag full of French bread that my father brought every day after work. At home, bread literally never ran out—there was always extra and it accumulated. So I went out with a very heavy bag of bread and walked that hallway that seemed eternal to me until I reached the gate, and there was another child like me, maybe the same age... I will never forget that day. That child received the bag from my hand and with a sparkle in his eyes told me, "Your mom is very good," and he got on the cart where his other brothers happily ate a piece of bread each along with their parents. I hadn't realized this fact, what my mother was doing—it was something simple but she was giving food to a poor and humble family for whom that might have been their meal or part of it that week. I could feel like Christ's disciples feeding that multitude, supposing there were many families. But this time it was one family, and that family's face changed because they could eat that day!
