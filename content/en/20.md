# 20 “Your Mom Is Very Good”

*“Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.”* — Matthew 25:40

There was a specific moment each week when, without ever failing, a horse-drawn cart would arrive at the corridor gate of my grandparents’ apartment in the Prado neighborhood of Montevideo.

It was a humble family dedicated to collecting: sometimes the cart came full of garbage bags, other times loaded with cardboard, and on occasions with bottles they collected house by house and from the trash cans they found on their daily route.

## The Weekly Ritual

On one occasion, as usual, they stopped at the corridor door. I, who was small—perhaps near eight years old—saw them from a distance and ran to tell my mother that they were knocking at the gate.

She called me, I went into the house, and she handed me a large bag full of baguettes that my father brought home every day after work. At home, literally, bread never ran out; it overflowed and accumulated day after day.

Then I went out carrying that very heavy bag, full of loaves. I walked down that corridor that to my short legs seemed endless, until I finally reached the gate.

## The Encounter That Marked My Life

There stood another child, like me, perhaps the same age. His eyes reflected a mixture of hope and need that I had never seen before.

I will never forget that day so significant. That child received from my hands the heavy bag, and with a special light in his eyes, he said to me with absolute sincerity: “Your mom is very good.”

Then he climbed onto the cart, where his other siblings waited anxiously. I saw them all, together with their parents, share with joy a piece of bread each. Their faces were completely transformed at having that food.

## The Awakening of Awareness

I had not realized until that moment the magnitude of what my mother was doing. For her it was something simple and natural, but in reality she was feeding a poor and humble family for whom that bread might be their main food, or a large part of it, during that week.

In that moment I could feel like the disciples of Christ when they fed the multitudes. Although it was not many families, but only one, that family had changed their expression of hope into abundance by being able to eat with dignity that day.

## The Silent Lesson

My mother never explained to me why she did this; she never told me it was an act of charity or Christian service. She simply did it, week after week, with the naturalness of one who understands that sharing is an essential part of life.

That child, with his innocent but deep words, taught me to see my mother with new eyes. I saw in her pure goodness, selfless love, the silent compassion that does not seek recognition but simply to relieve the suffering of others.

## The Treasure of the Soul

Years later I understood that my mother was living the words of the Savior: when she fed that hungry family, she was feeding Him. When she showed compassion to those in need, she was showing it to Christ Himself.

That day I learned that true goodness does not announce itself, does not proclaim itself, does not seek applause. True goodness simply is: it flows naturally from a heart that has understood that we are all brothers and sisters, that we all deserve dignity, that we all need love.

The phrase of that child—“Your mom is very good”—became one of the most precious treasures of my soul. It taught me that goodness is measured not by the greatness of the acts, but by the constancy of love; not by what we give, but by how we give.

And that sometimes the most sincere and true compliments come from the purest hearts: those of children who recognize goodness without filters, without pretension, with the absolute honesty of one who simply says what they feel.

That was my first lesson about the true Gospel in action.
