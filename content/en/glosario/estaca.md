# Stake

## Definition
A **stake** is a geographic organizational unit of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, generally composed of between 5 and 12 wards or branches. It is equivalent to a diocese in other Christian organizations.

## Organizational structure

### **Stake leadership**
- **Stake president**: Principal leader; he holds the keys of the priesthood
- **Two counselors**: Assist the president in his responsibilities
- **Stake secretary**: Handles records and official correspondence
- **Twelve high councilors**: Assist in the administration of the wards

### **Auxiliary organizations**
- **Stake Relief Society**: Organization for adult women
- **Stake Sunday School**: Teaching programs
- **Young Men organization**: For males ages 12–18
- **Young Women organization**: For young women ages 12–18
- **Stake Primary**: Organization for children

## Principal responsibilities

### **Pastoral supervision**
- Oversee the spiritual and temporal welfare of the members
- Coordinate with bishops of individual wards
- Provide leadership and doctrinal guidance
- Conduct worthiness interviews and call leaders

### **Ecclesiastical administration**
- Organize semiannual stake conferences
- Coordinate Church programs at the regional level
- Administer stake resources and budgets
- Facilitate temple and family history work

### **Leadership development**
- Train ward leaders
- Call and set apart presidents of auxiliary organizations
- Provide guidance in principles of leadership
- Coordinate training programs

## Stake activities and programs

### **Stake conferences**
- **General session**: For all members of the stake
- **Priesthood session**: Only for priesthood holders
- **Leadership session**: Training for local leaders
- **Frequency**: Generally twice a year

### **Youth programs**
- Appropriate dances and social activities
- Camps and outdoor activities
- Community service programs
- Sports and cultural events

### **Specialized services**
- Welfare and self-reliance programs
- Employment and education services
- Family history and genealogy program
- Coordination of missionary work

## Geography and demographics

### **Requirements to form a stake**
- A minimum of approximately 3,000 members
- At least 5 organized wards
- Sufficient qualified priesthood leadership
- Geographic and economic stability in the region

### **Stake centers**
- **Central buildings**: Generally include larger chapels
- **Training facilities**: Rooms for leadership meetings
- **Administrative offices**: For the presidency and organizations
- **Family history centers**: Genealogical resources

## Spiritual purpose

### **Strengthening of testimony**
- Inspiring conferences with Area leaders
- Opportunities for service and spiritual growth
- Coordinated ministering programs
- Activities that build faith

### **Unity and brotherhood**
- Creating bonds among neighboring wards
- Fostering lasting friendships among members
- Intergenerational programs that strengthen families
- Coordinated service in the community

## Historical development
The stake system was established by the prophet Joseph Smith as part of the Restoration of the gospel. The term comes from [Isaiah 54:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/54?lang=eng&id=p2#p2): "Enlarge the place of thy tent... lengthen thy cords, and strengthen thy **stakes**," symbolizing the strengthening and expansion of Zion.

Stakes represent the growth and organizational maturity of the Church, providing a stable structure for the pastoral care and spiritual development of the Saints throughout the world.
