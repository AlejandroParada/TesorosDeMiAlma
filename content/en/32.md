32 That Mountain

I was about to finish my mission, and I was in the city of Talca, and my area reached to the mountains at that time, the Andes Mountain Range.

Talca was peculiar—when they found out I was Uruguayan, there was always an affectionate hug. I think it was the place where I received the most affection of my entire mission, especially the elderly people at that time wanted to give me a hug. I didn't understand why this phenomenon occurred.

The mission leader wanted to take us to see a park that was toward the mountain range, a trip that was long. The mission leader said that "this moment will be very special, especially for you who are Uruguayan," he told me. I was left with curiosity. We arrived at the place, Radal Siete Tazas National Park, where the Bride's Veil waterfall was located.

"Many times those of us from Talca come to this place to meditate and pray," said the mission leader. "If you can look toward the northeast, you can see the mountains of the Cordillera. If you look carefully, over there you can see the Peteroa volcano, but to the right a few kilometers away was where the plane crashed." "Which plane crashed?" was my question. "The plane with the Uruguayans who were coming to play a rugby match." I recognize that at that moment I wasn't aware of the event—I didn't know the details. I couldn't imagine that I would get so close to that place (although we were perhaps 100 km from the exact location toward the cordillera). From that moment it became a holy place for me. I could understand why the people of Talca appreciated Uruguayans—they were a symbol of faith and hope, and I felt their love.

Years later, from books and movies, I can say that I was close to there, I knew the people of the place, and when they see a Uruguayan, love and compassion arise. I recognize that many doors opened, but when they saw I was Uruguayan, more hearts opened.
