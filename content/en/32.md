# 32 That Mountain

> **"How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace, that bringeth good tidings of good, that publisheth salvation, that saith unto Zion, Thy God reigneth!"** ([Mosiah 15:18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/15?lang=eng&id=p18#p18))

> **"I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help. My help cometh from the Lord, which made heaven and earth."** ([Psalms 121:1-2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/121?lang=eng&id=p1-p2#p1))

> **"For I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat: I was thirsty, and ye gave me drink: I was a stranger, and ye took me in."** ([Matthew 25:35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/25?lang=eng&id=p35#p35))

## The last days of my mission

I was about to finish my mission in Chile, and I was serving in the beautiful city of Talca. My area of labor extended to the majestic mountains of the Andes, that imposing mountain chain that forms the backbone of the South American continent.

Talca turned out to be a very peculiar place for me. When people learned that I was Uruguayan, there invariably came a warm embrace, a special smile, a warmth that went beyond ordinary courtesy. I sincerely believe it was the place where I received the most love and affection of my entire mission.

## The mystery of Chilean affection

Especially the older people of that time wanted to give me a hug when they heard my Uruguayan accent. It was a phenomenon that intrigued me deeply, but that I could not fully understand. Why this such special reaction? What was there about being Uruguayan that awakened so much tenderness in Chilean hearts?

I did not understand why this extraordinary phenomenon happened, but I received it with gratitude, sensing that there was something deeper behind so much spontaneous love.

## An excursion that would change my perspective

The ward mission leader wanted to take us to see a national park that lay on the way toward the mountains. It was a long trip, winding along mountain roads that carried us ever higher toward the Andes.

Before we left, the ward mission leader said to me with a mysterious smile: "This moment will be very special, and especially for you, who are Uruguayan." His words filled me with intrigue. What was there in that place that would have a special connection with me?

## Radal Siete Tazas National Park

We finally arrived at our destination: Radal Siete Tazas National Park, a place of extraordinary natural beauty where the waterfall known as "Velo de la Novia" is found. The landscape was simply overwhelming: imposing mountains, native forests, and the constant sound of water falling from the heights.

"Many times those of us from Talca come to this place to meditate and pray," the ward mission leader explained to me as we contemplated the magnificence of God's creation. His words resonated in my heart, because there was indeed something deeply spiritual in that setting.

## The revelation that changed everything

Then the ward mission leader pointed toward the mountains and said to me: "If you look toward the northeast, you can see the mountains of the cordillera. If you look carefully, over there you can see the Peteroa volcano, but to the right, a few kilometers away, is where the plane crashed."

"Which plane crashed?" was my immediate question, although in my heart I was already beginning to sense the answer.

"The plane with the Uruguayans who were coming to play a rugby match," he answered solemnly.

## The moment of understanding

I recognize that in that moment I was not fully aware of all the details of the event. I knew vaguely of the tragedy, but I did not know the whole story. I had never imagined that during my mission I would come so close to that sacred place (although we were perhaps 100 kilometers from the exact place of the impact, toward the deepest part of the mountains).

## A holy place

From that moment, that place became a holy place for me. Suddenly, everything made sense. I could finally understand why the people of Talca appreciated Uruguayans so much. It was not only courtesy or curiosity: for them, Uruguayans had become a living symbol of faith, hope, survival, and human strength.

I felt their love in a new and deep way. It was a love born of compassion, of respect, and of admiration for the courage and endurance those young men had shown in the mountains.

## The story the world came to know

Years later, through books, films, and documentaries, the whole world would come to know the incredible story of survival of the survivors of the Andes. The story of how 16 young Uruguayans survived 72 days in the most extreme conditions imaginable, keeping hope when all seemed lost, caring for one another, praying together, and finally finding the way back to civilization.

## The privilege of having been near

I can say with reverence that I was near that sacred place. I met the people of the area, I breathed the same mountain air, I contemplated the same summits that those brave young men saw during their terrible but inspiring experience.

## Open hearts

When the people of Talca saw a Uruguayan, love and compassion arose naturally. I recognize that during my mission many doors opened, but when they discovered that I was Uruguayan, even more hearts opened.

It was as if every Uruguayan carried with him a little of that story of survival, of that extraordinary demonstration of the human spirit that refuses to surrender to adversity.

## Lessons of faith in the mountains

### **Survival as a testimony**
Those young Uruguayans, without knowing it, became a living testimony that the human spirit, strengthened by faith and brotherly love, can overcome the most desperate circumstances.

### **Prayer in the heights**
In those mountains, far from civilization, those young men learned to pray with an intensity and sincerity that few of us experience. Their faith was refined in the crucible of suffering.

### **The love that sustains life**
The survival of those young men depended not only on their physical endurance, but on the love they had for one another. They cared for each other, they shared everything, and they never abandoned hope.

## The lasting impact

This experience in the Chilean mountains taught me that sometimes we are touched by stories of faith and survival in ways we do not expect. My mission took me physically near the place where one of the most extraordinary survival stories of the twentieth century was written.

## The mountain as a testimony

That mountain, majestic and relentless, had been a silent witness of suffering, but also of faith, hope, and brotherly love. The same summits that had witnessed pain and death had also seen miracles of survival and extraordinary examples of the power of the human spirit.

## Living scripture

As I contemplated those mountains, I remembered the words of the psalmist: **"I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help. My help cometh from the Lord, which made heaven and earth"** ([Psalms 121:1-2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/121?lang=eng&id=p1-p2#p1)). Those young Uruguayans had lifted their eyes to those same hills, seeking help, and they had found it.

## The legacy of the Andes

The legacy of those young Uruguayans transcends their extraordinary physical survival. Their story became a universal testimony that hope, faith, and brotherly love can sustain a human being even in the most extreme circumstances.

## A missionary reflection

As a missionary, I learned that sometimes the gospel is preached not only with words, but with examples of faith and endurance that touch hearts in a deep way. The story of those young men had prepared the way for love to flow more freely toward other Uruguayans.

## A prayer of gratitude

When I look back on that experience in the Chilean mountains, my heart fills with gratitude. Gratitude for having been near such a sacred place, for having felt the love of the people of Talca, and for having learned that mountains can be both places of trial and places of testimony.

That mountain taught me that true faith is forged in the heights, where the air is thin but the spirit rises, where circumstances are adverse but hope clings to life, where the cold is intense but brotherly love provides the warmth needed to survive.

The mountains are still there, majestic and eternal, keeping the secrets of faith, survival, and love that were written on their slopes so many years ago.
