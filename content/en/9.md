# 9 Atilio's Faith

When I found out that Atilio was hospitalized at Hospital de Clínicas, I went immediately. Back then, before the pandemic (2019), I worked nearby and could walk to the hospital.

When I went, they were doing several tests on him and some family member was always present.

The Diagnosis

One of those days when I arrived in the afternoon, the test results had come in—nothing positive, it was cancer. Then the doctor came to give the interpretation of the clinical analyses. Atilio said, "Doctor, don't hide anything, tell me the whole truth, whatever it is I leave it in God's hands, just tell me." The doctor told him, "Look, you have a tumor in the upper part of your kidney. We don't know what size it is. If we operate, we don't know what we might find. If it's small, we'll only remove that part, otherwise I'll have to remove the kidney." I had never seen such faith as when Atilio responded, "Doctor, I trust you and I know you'll do your best, do what you have to do!" What surrender and confidence.

The operation would be in two days. After he left the room, Atilio and I stayed there with his daughter, and I told him, after feeling a strong impression in my heart, "I want you to know, Atilio, that I'm going to fast for you so everything goes well, starting today." His daughter also said she would join the proposal and would fast as well.

The operation was a success, and although they did have to remove his kidney, the operation went very well without complications. It was considered "the major operation that came out impeccable and very well, to the amazement of the doctors." I wondered if the doctor knew we had fasted for the operation to go well—I even asked Atilio if the doctor knew anything.

Atilio told me, "You don't know what happened the day before the operation. The doctor, after finishing his shift, came to see me and said: 'I see that you are a man of faith—I'm going to fast for you!'" I had never heard anything like that in my life—a doctor fasting for a patient.

Atilio lived several more years and blessed the lives of his family and other people in very special ways.

One day I was at the Montevideo Temple, waiting for a session to begin (a meeting we participate in like the ones at the chapel on Sundays), and when I saw several people enter, one seemed familiar to me—it was the doctor who had operated on Atilio. I had the joy of seeing him there. I knew that God was mindful of all the details, as He always is.
