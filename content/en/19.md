# The First Inhabitants of America

> **"For behold, the Lord doth grant unto all nations, of their own nation and tongue, to teach his word, yea, in wisdom, all that he seeth fit that they should have."** ([Alma 29:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/29?lang=eng&id=p8#p8))

> **"Know ye not that I, the Lord your God, have created all men, and that I remember those who are upon the isles of the sea; and that I rule in the heavens above and in the earth beneath; and I bring forth my word unto the children of men, yea, even upon all the nations of the earth?"** ([2 Nephi 29:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/29?lang=eng&id=p7#p7))

## A young altar boy in the 1940s

When my father was a child, he attended catechism classes and helped as an altar boy in a parish. It was the 1940s in Uruguay, a time when Catholic faith shaped the daily life of many families, and catechism classes were a natural part of childhood.

## An unexpected lesson

One day, the parish priest who taught them shared something my father would never forget. He spoke to them about the first inhabitants of America. He told them that the ancestors of the Indigenous peoples were called Nephites and Lamanites, and that they had come from a very distant land in the East.

In that simple classroom, without copies of the Book of Mormon or missionaries of the restored Church, a Catholic priest was telling a story that years later my father would recognize as part of the sacred scriptures.

## Seeds sown before the harvest

That was how my father learned the story of the Nephites and the Lamanites long before the Gospel of Jesus Christ arrived in Uruguay in 1947.

God was preparing the way. When the preaching of the restored Gospel finally came, there were hearts that had already heard, even if imperfectly, echoes of an ancient truth. It was no coincidence: the Lord prepares His children in many ways, sometimes by paths we would never imagine.

## God's hand in history

This experience taught me that truth is not locked inside a single place or a single tradition. God can use people of different beliefs to plant seeds of understanding. That priest, without fully knowing it, was opening a door in the mind of a Uruguayan boy who, in time, would come to embrace the fulness of the Gospel.

The Book of Mormon was not foreign to America: it spoke of this continent, of its ancient inhabitants, and of God's promise to their descendants. And my father, while still young, had already heard part of that account in a catechism class.

## A testimony of divine preparation

When I remember this story, I feel gratitude. I see the Lord's hand preparing a generation: first with a word spoken in a parish, then with the arrival of the missionaries, and finally with the conversion of hearts ready to receive more light.

Thus I learned that God does not improvise. He sows in advance, waters with patience, and harvests in His own due time. And sometimes the first seed arrives wrapped in a catechism lesson, years before the restored Gospel fully blossoms in a land.
