3 Standing by My Bedside

"Can you still sleep when the sea threatens to plunge us into vast depths?" (Be Still, My Soul, hymn 54)

In our neighborhood there was a loving Relief Society president, Sister Cecilia, very attentive and helpful. She had her family of teenage and grown children and took care of household tasks. Although she was very busy, there was always "a little place" to be there, to hold a hand, give a hug, say "I love you."

With the friends from the block and with my "new skill" (I knew how to ride a bicycle), we would cruise our designated space (100 meters) of the sidewalk, looking for new ways to pass the time with our bicycles.

I remember one day when my mother left early for the market that morning, and we were left playing on the block with our bicycles. But my memories of that day are very blurry or hazy. I remember it being like a dream where everything darkened around me. I have a vague memory of feeling surrounded by my friends, then surrounded by my neighbors—everyone was talking to me but I couldn't understand them. I remember being carried and seeing several cars pass by, and that a soft drink delivery truck stopped in front of me.

Then I don't remember anything else. That's when I remember being in a special place with lots of light that was extremely beautiful, where there were beings of light who approached me. Again I felt surrounded by people with light and shining robes. I knew they were angels who were holding me up. They spoke to me but I don't remember what they said—I couldn't make out their faces, which seemed very familiar to me. I felt great peace. I remember that they all stepped aside and one remained, and I could clearly feel and understand that they were leaving me in the care of an angel who would be watching over me.

The light began to diminish gradually, and I could see the angel who had remained, and I remember its great light. The light of the place continued to reduce its brightness until only the light of that angel remained, and the light continued diminishing toward the person of that angel, and I paid attention to its face that I couldn't see well, but I am sure I know that face.

When the light allowed me to see, I could see... it was Sister Cecilia who had been taking care of me. When she realized I was waking up, she said "Ale, you're back!" very emotionally. I don't know how long I was in the hospital, but she took turns with my mother and others, as I later found out.

I know that there are angels very close by—of that I am sure. They care for us and watch over us.

Later I found out that I had had an accident and hit my head against a column. I had a skull fracture with loss of consciousness. I also lost the memories of the entire morning—I didn't know what happened or what I did, how I got to the hospital. The last thing I remember was my mother leaving for the market.

I learned that everyone tried to save me. My friends carried me together trying to get me to respond. My friends' parents also tried to get me to react, but I remained unconscious until my mother arrived from the market. She carried me in her arms to the street seeking help for her son. No one wanted to stop until a soft drink truck stopped and took me to the nearest hospital.

Sometimes it's our turn to give, other times it's our turn to receive, as it was for me in that moment.
