# 3 Standing Beside My Bed

*"Canst thou still sleep when the sea threatens to plunge us into vast depths?"* — Hymn “Master, the Tempest Is Raging”

In our ward there was a loving Relief Society president, Sister Cecilia. She was a very attentive and helpful woman who, although she had her own family of teenage and grown children and took care of every household task, was always very busy yet invariably found “a little spot” to be present—to hold a hand, give a warm hug, or simply say “I love you” with sincerity.

With the friends from the block, and with my “new skill” (I already knew how to ride a bicycle thanks to Sergio), we would cruise our marked territory of about a hundred meters of sidewalk, constantly looking for new ways to pass the time and have fun with our bikes.

One day I remember vividly, my mother left early for the market, as was her custom, and we stayed playing on the block with the bicycles. Yet my memories of that day are very cloudy and scattered, like fragmented pieces of a confused dream.

I remember that suddenly everything began to grow dark around me, as if a shadow wrapped itself around my world. I have a vague recollection of feeling surrounded first by my friends, then by my neighbors. Everyone was talking to me, but I could not understand what they were saying, as if their voices came from very far away. I vaguely remember being carried, seeing several cars pass quickly by, and a soft-drink delivery truck stopping in front of me.

Then I simply remember nothing more of this earthly world.

That is when I remember being in a completely special place, filled with an indescribable light and exceedingly beautiful. There were people of light who came near me with love and tenderness. Again I felt surrounded, but this time by luminous beings dressed in shining robes that glowed with a light of their own. I knew immediately, without any doubt, that they were angels who were holding me up and protecting me.

They spoke to me, but I do not remember exactly what they said. I could not clearly distinguish their faces, though in some way they were very known and familiar to me. What I do remember perfectly is the feeling of a deep and inexplicable peace that filled my whole being.

I remember that they all gradually stepped aside until only one remained. I could clearly feel, with a certainty that admitted no doubt, that they were leaving me in the care of that special angel who would watch over me.

The light began to diminish gradually, allowing me to see better the angel who had stayed with me. I vividly remember his great radiance. The light of the place kept reducing its intensity until only the light emanating from that guardian angel remained. The brightness continued concentrating toward the person of that heavenly being, and I paid close attention to his face, which I could not see with complete clarity, yet I was absolutely sure that I knew that beloved face.

When at last the light allowed me to see with earthly clarity, I could tell that it was Sister Cecilia who had been faithfully caring for me. Realizing that I was waking up, she exclaimed with great emotion: “Ale, you’re back!”

I do not know exactly how long I was in the hospital, but afterward I learned that she had taken turns with my mother and other loved ones to care for me during my unconsciousness.

I know with absolute certainty that there are angels very near us. Of that I am completely sure. They care for us, protect us, and watch over us with infinite love.

Later they told me the details of the accident: I had violently struck my head against a post, suffering a traumatic brain injury with loss of consciousness. In addition, I lost all memory of that morning; I did not know exactly how the accident happened, what I had been doing before, or how I arrived at the hospital. The last thing I clearly remembered was seeing my mother leave for the market.

I learned that everyone had tried desperately to help me. My friends carried me among them, trying to get me to respond. My friends’ parents also did all they could to wake me, but I remained unconscious. When my mother finally arrived from the market and saw what had happened, she took me in her arms and ran out into the street, urgently seeking help for her son. No one wanted to stop to help, until miraculously a soft-drink truck stopped and took me to the nearest hospital.

Sometimes in life it is our turn to give, and other times it is our turn to receive, as happened to me in that critical moment. But always, always, we are surrounded by the love of our Heavenly Father and of His ministering angels.
