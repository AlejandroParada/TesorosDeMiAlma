31 Wrapped with Love

When I was in Primary, there was an elderly sister who took care of the nursery children. She had a special light, and her love for children transcended barriers. Several generations loved Sister Lila Sánchez, very low-profile—she was the grandmother everyone wanted to have.

She was alone, had never married, and she liked to knit and embroider. Since time immemorial she had made embroideries that became very popular among the members of our neighborhood. I think there was no one from that era who didn't have something made by Lila, because when visiting families' homes I would discover—whether a small tablecloth on a table, a tapestry on the wall, or a pillow on the couch—it was easy to tell "Lila was here."

When I was in Primary, Lila told me that when I went on my mission, she was going to give me one of her knitted items.

At a time when missionaries took their own clothes and coats, some traditions had become established in the neighborhood. Many of those who went on missions received a blanket from Lila Sánchez, or a scarf, or a hat, or a sweater—especially her Primary students.

Time passed, as it does in life, and during my adolescence Lila's time came to depart. An entire neighborhood had to live through the bitterness of her departure, but she had left a mark on the lives of generations.

Years later the time came to leave on my mission (at age 19). On the day of my farewell, the Relief Society sisters brought a large package, a gift. I was able to open it, and inside was a blanket that had been carefully crafted. The sisters told me they had cared for it and kept it all this time for this day, because it had been her wish. When I examined it I noticed something—the blanket had not been finished, because it was my blanket that she was working on when her time came to depart. Knowing this caused me great emotion—that she didn't want to leave without first leaving me her gift for the mission, a carefully embroidered blanket.

Times have changed and blankets are not taken on missions, but rather the minimum of personal items and clothing. That blanket symbolized a legacy of love and faithfulness, and in time perhaps someone very needy received that blanket, but what it left in my soul is that love and care endure forever.
