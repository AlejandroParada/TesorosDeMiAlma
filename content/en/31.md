# 31 Wrapped in Love

> **"Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up, doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil."** ([1 Corinthians 13:4-5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/13?lang=eng&id=p4-p5#p4))

> **"Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering."** ([Colossians 3:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/col/3?lang=eng&id=p12#p12))

## Sister Lila Sánchez: an angel in Primary

When I was in Primary, there was an older sister who cared for the children in the nursery with extraordinary dedication. Her name was Lila Sánchez, and she had a special light that radiated from her. Her love for the children crossed every imaginable barrier: of age, of personality, of family circumstances.

Several entire generations came to love Sister Lila Sánchez deeply. She was a woman of very low profile; she never sought recognition or attention, but we all considered her the grandmother we all wished we had. She had that serene wisdom and that unconditional love that only souls truly consecrated to service possess.

## A life dedicated to the art of love

Sister Lila had remained single, but far from feeling empty, she had filled her life with purpose and service. She loved to knit and embroider, and from time immemorial she had devoted herself to creating beautiful embroideries that became legendary among the members of our ward.

I sincerely believe there was not a single family of that time that did not have something made by Lila's loving hands. When visiting the homes of the families in the ward, I could always discover her mark: whether a delicately embroidered little tablecloth on a table, an artistically woven tapestry on the wall, or a carefully made cushion on the sofa. It was easy to tell: "Lila has been here."

## A promise that my heart kept

When I was about eight years old, during my days in Primary, Sister Lila made me a promise that would be engraved forever in my memory. With that sweet smile that was so characteristic of her, she said to me: "When you go on your mission, I am going to give you one of my knitted pieces."

Her words were spoken with such certainty and love that I did not doubt for a moment that she would keep her promise. For an eight-year-old boy, the mission seemed as far away as eternity, but Sister Lila was already planning to bless my future service to the Lord.

## A tradition of missionary love

In those days, missionaries had the responsibility of taking their own clothes and coats to face the varied climates of their fields of labor. In our ward a beautiful tradition had been established: many of the young men who left to serve missions received a blanket from Sister Lila Sánchez, or a scarf, or a hat, or a sweater.

Especially her beloved Primary students were blessed with these gifts made with love. Each stitch represented a prayer; each interwoven thread symbolized the motherly care she felt for these future servants of the Lord.

## The departure of an angel

Time passed, as it inevitably does in life, and during my adolescence Sister Lila was called to the other side. The whole ward had to live the bitterness of her departure. It was as if a light that had illuminated our lives for decades had gone out.

Yet, although her physical presence was no longer with us, she had left an indelible mark on the lives of entire generations. Her legacy of love and service would go on living in the hearts of all who had been touched by her kindness.

## The fulfillment of an eternal promise

Years later, the moment finally came to leave on my mission (at age 19). On the day of my farewell, a moment I had anticipated for years, the Relief Society sisters came forward with a large package, carefully wrapped.

"This is a special gift," they told me with emotional smiles. When I opened it, my eyes filled with tears: inside there was a beautifully made blanket, knitted with the mastery and love that only Sister Lila's hands could create.

## The moving discovery

The sisters explained to me that they had cared for and kept this blanket all this time for this exact day, because it had been Sister Lila's expressed wish before she passed. But when I examined it more closely, I noticed something that caused me an indescribable emotion: the blanket had not been completely finished.

Then I understood the moving truth: this was **my** blanket, the specific blanket she had been working on when she was called to the other side. Knowing that filled me with a deep and sacred emotion. She had not wanted to leave without first making sure that her gift for my mission was ready, a blanket carefully embroidered with all her love.

## The eternal legacy of love

Times have changed significantly since then. Now missionaries do not take blankets on their missions, but only the minimum of personal items and necessary clothing. That blanket, however, symbolized something much deeper than a simple article of warmth: it represented a legacy of love and faithfulness that transcends time and death.

Over the years, perhaps someone in great need physically received that blanket to keep warm against the cold. But what truly remained in my soul is the deep understanding that genuine love and care endure forever.

## Lessons from a consecrated life

### **Silent service**
Sister Lila never sought recognition or applause. Her service was silent but constant, like the stitches of her embroidery: one after another, day after day, creating beauty and providing warmth.

### **Love that transcends death**
Although Sister Lila had passed away, her love continued to manifest itself years later through that unfinished blanket. True love knows no limitations of time or death.

### **Sacred promises**
A promise made with sincere love has power to bless lives even after the one who made it is no longer physically present. Sister Lila kept her word from the other side.

### **Individual ministry**
Every Primary child was special to Sister Lila. She saw missionary potential in eight-year-old children and was already preparing blessings for their future service to the Lord.

## Living scripture

Sister Lila Sánchez was living scripture. In her were fulfilled Paul's words about charity: she suffered long, she was kind, she sought not her own, she was not easily provoked. Her whole life was a silent but powerful testimony of the gospel of Jesus Christ.

## The covering that never wears out

Physically, that blanket may have been used and eventually worn out. But the true covering I received from Sister Lila—her love, her example, her faithfulness—that covering never wears out. It still warms my soul decades later.

## A prayer of gratitude

When I reflect on Sister Lila Sánchez, my heart fills with gratitude to the Lord for placing **angels** like her on our path. She was one of those special people who make life more beautiful, warmer, and fuller of hope.

## The call to be like Lila

Her example challenges me to ask myself: Am I creating legacies of love that will bless lives even after I am gone? Are there young people in my ward who will benefit from my service and care? Am I weaving blessings into the lives of others with the same dedication with which Sister Lila knitted her blankets?

## The eternal treasure

Sister Lila Sánchez taught me that true treasures are not the material things we accumulate, but the love we give and the lives we bless. Her blanket became a tangible symbol of an eternal truth: when we serve with pure love, we create legacies that endure beyond this life.

Every time I feel cold in my soul, I remember the warmth of Sister Lila's love, and I realize that the truly blessed are those of us who have been **wrapped in love** by souls like hers.
