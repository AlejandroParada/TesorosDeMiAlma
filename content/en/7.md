# 7 César at Tres Cruces

"...I was a stranger and you took me in;" (Matthew 25:35)

On one occasion I was going through several problems that I considered important and big, so I decided to fast. At that time I was teaching computer classes in Montevideo a few days a week and in Minas twice a week. I lived outside Montevideo, and to get to Minas I had to pass through Tres Cruces. One day they asked me if I could go to Minas to cover for another teacher. This schedule change made it inconvenient for me to return home, so I stayed at the terminal since the next day I would have to go to Minas, and I had already taught that day. So the day I was already fasting was when I stayed at the terminal until the next day when I would have to go to Minas. I would sleep at the terminal, I was fasting, praying, and was about to end the fast (24 hours), but sitting in the terminal seats reflecting, I felt like my prayers weren't getting past the terminal ceiling, or it was my impression that I wasn't receiving an answer. I wanted my prayers to reach "Heaven" but felt like they weren't getting there, so I decided to continue the fast. As soon as I made that decision, thoughts began to invade me: "You're crazy... you won't last... you're going to die here..." But I didn't pay attention to them and said, "My prayers have to get past the pipes in the terminal ceiling. God is not going to let me die here and will answer my prayers."

The morning of the next day arrived... I felt hunger during my dozing, thinking "If they gave me a cooked elephant I would eat it all"... when I felt someone talking to me: "Alejandro, wake up! Alejandro, wake up!" When I opened my eyes I looked around, and even in the seats next to me there was no one—there hadn't been anyone else during the night. But then I looked down and next to my leg I found a paper bag carefully prepared with two hamburgers inside. I was amazed that someone had forgotten them—I touched them and they were freshly made! I said to myself, "Father, I haven't finished the fast yet," and I clearly felt, "Look for him, look for him because he cries out to me!" I thought they're not for me, they're for someone else. So I stood up and began looking at the people sitting nearby—there weren't many—and I still felt "Look for him, because he cries out to me." I kept walking, looking at the people there. I kept walking until I found a very elderly man, somewhat hunched over, sitting against the windows with no one near him. "It's him," I felt. I went and sat next to him. He had a horrible smell—you could tell he hadn't washed in a long time. Even so, I woke him up and he was surprised that I approached him. I told him, "I came to give you this—I was sent by God." The man, somewhat through tears, thanked me and told me he had said to God, "If you exist, you won't let me die here—today I will let myself die." "You don't have to die here. I brought you this so you could eat," and I gave him a hamburger from the bag. I began talking with César—he told me his name. He told me they had evicted him and he had nowhere to go or live and was living at the terminal. "I'm going to help you," I told him. "All this will pass." I started moving to help César. I went to MIDES to present his situation. From MIDES they went to look for him and took care of him. They even helped him get out of there, put him in a home, and covered his needs. I made sure to follow up on his condition. The times I went through Tres Cruces I brought him clothes or food, until they were able to place him in a home.

If you ask me what happened to your important problems and worries that you were fasting for—what became of them?—they were reduced in intensity and importance. I realized they could be solved and they were, in time. But by fasting I aligned myself with God's will, and I was able to bless someone else's life. In doing so, my problems were also being resolved.

When I went to MIDES to verify that everything went well the last time, they asked me, "Alejandro, what are you to César? Are you his relative?" No. "Are you his neighbor?" No. "Are you his friend?" No. "Then what is he to you?" Because we need to register it in the system. "I want you to put that César is my neighbor."
