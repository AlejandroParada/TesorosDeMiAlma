# 7 César at Tres Cruces

*“I was a stranger, and ye took me in: Naked, and ye clothed me: I was sick, and ye visited me: I was in prison, and ye came unto me.”* — [Matthew 25:35-36](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/25?lang=eng&id=p35-p36#p35)

There was a time when I was going through several problems that, to me, were important and large, so I decided to fast in order to seek divine direction. In those days I was teaching computer classes in Montevideo a few days a week and in Minas twice a week. I lived outside Montevideo, and to get to Minas I necessarily had to pass through Tres Cruces.

One day they asked if I could go to Minas an extra day to cover for a teacher. That change of schedule made it impractical to go home; it was better to stay at the terminal, since the next day I would have to travel to Minas again, and I was already coming from teaching that same day.

So, on the day I was already fasting, I decided to stay at the Tres Cruces terminal until the next day, when I was to leave for Minas. I would sleep right there, continuing my fast and my prayer.

I sat in the terminal seats, reflecting. I had already fasted for twenty-four hours, but I felt as if my prayers could not get past the terminal ceiling. My impression was that I was receiving no answer, and I fervently wanted my prayers to reach “heaven.” Feeling that they were not getting through, I decided to continue the fast.

It was when I made that decision that discouraging thoughts began to crowd in on me: “You’re crazy… you won’t hold out… you’re going to die right here…”

But I paid them no attention and said to myself with determination: “My prayers have to pass through the pipes in this terminal’s ceiling. God is not going to let me die here, and He will answer my prayers.”

The next morning came. I felt an intense hunger through my fitful sleep. I thought: “If they handed me a cooked elephant, I would eat the whole thing.”

Suddenly I clearly felt someone speaking to me: “Alejandro, wake up! Alejandro, wake up!”

When I opened my eyes, I looked around. Even the seats beside me were empty, and no one else had been there all night. Then I looked down, and beside my leg I found a carefully prepared paper bag containing two hamburgers.

I was astonished that someone had “forgotten” them. I touched them, and they were freshly made. I said to myself: “Father, I have not finished the fast yet.”

Then I clearly felt: “Look for him! Look for him, for he is crying out to me!”

I thought: “These are not for me. They are for someone else.” So I stood up and began to watch the few people sitting nearby. I still felt the impression: “Look for him, for he is crying out to me!”

I kept walking, looking at the people in the terminal. I went on until I found a very elderly man, half curled up, sitting against the windows, completely alone.

“It is him,” I felt at once.

I went and sat down beside him. He had a very strong odor; it was plain he had not washed in a long time. Even so, I woke him gently, and he was surprised that I had drawn near.

I said to him: “I came to give you this. I was sent by God.”

The man, with tears in his eyes, thanked me from the heart. Then he said something that shook me: “I had told God, ‘If You exist, You will not let me die here. Today I will let myself die.’”

“You do not have to die here,” I answered. “I brought you this so you could eat.” And I gave him a hamburger from the bag.

I began to talk with César — that is the name he told me. He said he had been swindled, that he had nowhere to go and nowhere to live, and that he was surviving in the terminal.

“I am going to help you,” I promised him. “All of this will pass.”

I set to work at once to help César. I went to MIDES to lay his situation before them. From MIDES they went to find him and took his case to heart. They helped him out of that situation, placed him in a suitable home, and covered all his basic needs.

I took it upon myself to follow his condition constantly. Every time I passed through Tres Cruces I brought him clothes or food, until at last they were able to place him permanently in a home where he received the care he needed.

If someone were to ask me, “And what became of those important problems and worries you were fasting over? Where did they end up?”, my answer would be that they shrank, both in intensity and in importance. I realized they could be solved, and in time they were. But by fasting I aligned myself with the will of God, and I was able to bless someone else’s life. As I did, my own problems also began, little by little, to be resolved.

The last time I went to MIDES to make sure everything was in order, they asked me:

“Alejandro, what are you to César? Are you a relative?”

“No,” I answered.

“Do you live near him?”

“No.”

“Are you his friend?”

“No.”

“Then what is he to you? We need to register it in the system.”

My answer was immediate, and it came from the heart: “I want you to put down that César is my neighbor.”

That day I learned that when we sincerely seek the will of God through fasting and prayer, He not only answers our petitions; He uses us as instruments to answer the desperate prayers of others. And as we serve our neighbor, our own problems find perspective and a way forward.
