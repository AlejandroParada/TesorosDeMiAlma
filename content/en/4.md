# 4 The Tithing That Saved My Motorcycle

"...I will open the windows of heaven..."

On one occasion, I had received my salary, and I taught computer classes, traveling by motorcycle every day to teach in another part of Montevideo, covering several kilometers to get there.

Like many Latter-day Saints, the principle of tithing is an integral part of our lives—it's more a matter of faith than amounts and mathematics. When we put it first, everything else falls into place, managing to cover what we least expected, and we never end up, as we say in Uruguay, "a patas" (without resources to make it to the end of the month).

There are moments in life when we will be tested, extreme situations "...and we will prove them herewith, to see if they will do all things whatsoever the Lord their God shall command them..." (Abraham 3:25). And mine was no exception to the rule, when we have to choose whether or not to obey a commandment.

When riding the motorcycle, it requires maintenance—after a certain number of kilometers, it needs an oil change. When I reached the specific kilometers, it was necessary to change the oil. You could buy oil of different quality, a very economical quality or one of very good quality. When the time came to change the oil (the oil that an engine uses becomes so thick that if it's not changed in time it can blow the engine), it was the day I had been paid. It was an unexpected expense because I assumed I wouldn't reach those kilometers so quickly (I expected to do it later). In my budget I hadn't included changing the oil, and the budget elements were already assigned to basic bills (electricity, water, gas, fuel, etc.) that couldn't be missed.

For the first time in my life I was in the dilemma: "If I pay tithing I won't have enough to buy the oil, and if I buy the oil I won't have enough to pay tithing." I remembered the scripture: "...prove me now herewith, saith the Lord of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven..." (Malachi 3:10). On my knees I presented my situation to God and expressed my concern that "I don't have enough money..." So I decided to pay tithing to the bishop that next Sunday, trusting in God's mercies. On Monday I would have to go to work on the motorcycle.

The week began, and Monday arrived. I got up early in the morning and went to make preparations to go to work, I think already resigned to the future that awaited me, with the impossibility of being able to buy oil to make the change. When I approached the motorcycle, I saw next to it on the ground a bottle of motorcycle oil, and it wasn't just any oil—it was good quality. As soon as I saw it, I got back on my knees to thank Him for answering my prayer.

At the end of that day and with peace of mind, I changed the motorcycle oil, remembering that there is a God who cares for me and knows my needs, and if I obey the commandments I will be blessed.
