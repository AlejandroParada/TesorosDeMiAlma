# 4 The Tithe That Saved My Motorcycle

*“…I will open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it.”* — Malachi 3:10

At that time, I had just received my monthly paycheck. I taught computer classes and traveled by motorcycle every day to teach in another area of Montevideo, covering several kilometers to reach my destination.

As many Latter-day Saints know, the principle of tithing is an integral part of our spiritual life. It is a matter of faith far more than of amounts and mathematics. When we put it first, everything else falls into place miraculously, covering unexpected expenses and, as the Uruguayan saying goes, we never “end up flat broke” (without enough to make it to the end of the month).

There are moments in life when we will be tested—limiting situations where our faith is put to the proof: *“…and we will prove them herewith, to see if they will do all things whatsoever the Lord their God shall command them…”* (Abraham 3:25). My experience was no exception to this universal rule, when we have to choose whether or not to obey a divine commandment.

Constant use of a motorcycle requires regular maintenance. When you reach a certain specific number of kilometers, an oil change is absolutely necessary. You can buy oil of different qualities—from a very economical one to one of excellent quality—but the change is inevitable.

When the critical moment arrived to change the oil (used oil becomes so thick that, if it is not changed in time, it can literally seize the engine), it coincided exactly with the day I had received my salary. It was a completely unforeseen expense, because I had assumed I would not reach those kilometers so quickly and expected to be able to do it later.

In my monthly budget I had not allowed for this expense, and every line item was already meticulously assigned to the basic indispensable bills: electricity, water, gas, fuel, food, and other expenses that could not be skipped.

For the first time in my life I found myself in the most difficult dilemma of my experience as a Church member: “If I pay tithing, I won’t have enough to buy the oil, and if I buy the oil, I won’t have enough to pay a full tithe.”

I then remembered the promise of the scripture: *“Prove me now herewith, saith the Lord of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it”* (Malachi 3:10).

I knelt and humbly laid my financial situation before my Heavenly Father. I expressed my sincere concern that “I didn’t have enough money” for both things. After praying fervently, I decided to pay my tithing in full to the bishop that coming Sunday, trusting fully in the tender mercies of God. The following Monday I would necessarily have to go to work on the motorcycle.

A new week began. Monday came and I got up early in the morning to make the usual preparations to go to work. I think I was already mentally resigned to the uncertain future that awaited me, given the apparent impossibility of being able to buy the oil needed for that vital change.

As I approached my motorcycle to check it before leaving, something caught my attention. There, right beside it on the ground, was a completely new bottle of motorcycle oil. And it was not just any ordinary oil: it was of excellent quality, exactly the kind my engine needed.

As soon as I saw it, I immediately knelt again, this time to thank my Heavenly Father deeply for having answered my desperate prayer in such a direct and loving way.

At the end of that day, and with immense peace in my heart, I changed the oil in my motorcycle. As I did so, I remembered with gratitude that there is a God who cares for me personally, who knows my needs before I express them, and that if I faithfully obey His commandments, I will always be blessed in ways that surpass my understanding.

The windows of heaven had truly been opened, and the blessing had overflowed exactly as He had promised.
