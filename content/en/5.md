# 5 The Other Barbecue

"I was hungry, and you gave me food..." (Matthew 25:35)

It was the eve of May 1st and everyone was busy shopping to spend Workers' Day with a traditional barbecue. Although I didn't feel entirely well about celebrating something we had planned as a family, I had been laid off the day before, so I looked forward without letting sadness and discouragement invade me. I was shopping at the supermarket, at the butcher shop, and was about to order barbecue meat when I clearly felt the impression "buy another barbecue portion." "Okay, I'll do it," and I bought another portion of barbecue meat. I reflected, "I bought two barbecue portions, what else should I do?" and I thought, "Do you remember that low-income family from the neighborhood? They surely won't have barbecue on May 1st—well, take them the barbecue..."

So that evening I went to their house with "the surprise." When I gave them the barbecue meat, they had no words to thank me. I said, "I know it's not much, but everyone in your house will be able to eat." This day is not very different from the others for a low-income family—many times it's unimaginable to see barbecue on their table—but this day would be different for them. It would be a day of greater unity, because a meal is an occasion to bring the family closer together.
