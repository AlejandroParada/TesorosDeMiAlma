15 Can You Say the Prayer? (The Story of Livio "Tito" Basini at the Hospital)

"...and if they live they shall live unto me" (D&C 42:44)

A few years ago, a former bishop and very close family friend, Livio "Tito" Basini, was hospitalized at the mutual insurance clinic "La Española" (in Montevideo, Uruguay), and the brothers were coordinating to take turns staying overnight to care for him at the hospital. So I went and was able to meet his roommate as well, a man with great spirit and spark. They had something in common—both had been diagnosed with cancer. But what happened that day I will never forget—it marked my life forever. But let me tell the sequence of how it happened so you can understand why it was so special for me.

When I arrived in the afternoon, Tito introduced me to Roberto, his "room" companion, and the conversation went more or less like this:

Tito: "Roberto, this is my friend Alejandro," (said Tito), "he's also a member of the Church. Look, Roberto" (looking at me and addressing me) "is not a member but it's as if he were."

Roberto: "Oh, how nice! So he must be as good as you." (I blushed and said, well, it's not that much, haha)

After settling in for a while,

Roberto: "It's time to read the scriptures," he said. "Alejandro, can you read from First Nephi twelve, which is where we're at? Oh, but first we're going to have a prayer—do you feel up to saying it?"

"Sure," I responded, and I said the prayer, and then we began reading the Book of Mormon in that chapter.

I couldn't believe what was happening, and I looked at Tito amazed, and he nodded his head. The Spirit that was felt that night at that moment literally has no description. After some words of reflection, Roberto said (in which he said he was very grateful to be in that place, sick, because he wouldn't have known the true Gospel, and before he wouldn't have accepted it), then he said, "Now Tito, you can say the prayer and give thanks because Alejandro came." I couldn't hold back tears that night—I truly felt like the most blessed person in the world!

The next day, when I had to leave, they also just happened to tell them that they were discharging Tito, who would go home during the day after some routine paperwork (his family would take care of it).

So Tito spoke with Roberto and told him: "Roberto, you already know that when they discharge us, whether from 'here' or from this life, remember..." and Roberto interrupted him and said, "Yes, I already know—I must look for the missionaries so they can teach me, both here and on the other side too." I'm sure of that.

What words that reached me deeply in my soul! What immense conviction I learned that day from these two special men! I had never heard a person be grateful for getting sick (when he reflected on the scriptures we read), because he came to know the most important thing for life—the Gospel of Jesus Christ. What joy I felt that day, and I consider it a treasure for my soul!
