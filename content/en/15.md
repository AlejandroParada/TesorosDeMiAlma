# 15 “Can You Say the Prayer?”

*“And if they die they shall die unto me, and if they live they shall live unto me. For they that live shall live unto me, and they that die shall die unto me; for they are mine.”* — [Doctrine and Covenants 42:44](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/42?lang=eng&id=p44#p44)

Some years ago, a dear friend of my family and former bishop, Livio “Tito” Basini, was admitted to the “La Española” health cooperative in Montevideo. The brothers and sisters of the ward were coordinating to take turns spending the nights caring for him in the hospital.

That is how it came to be my turn one afternoon, and I also had the opportunity to meet Roberto, his roommate. He was a man full of energy and spark, with a very cheerful personality despite the circumstances. They had something painful in common: both had been diagnosed with cancer.

But what happened that day I will never forget. It marked my life forever in a deep and unexpected way. Allow me to relate the sequence of how everything unfolded, so that you can understand why it was so special to me.

## The Introductions

When I arrived in the afternoon, Tito introduced me to Roberto, his roommate. More or less this was the initial conversation:

**Tito**: “Roberto, this is my friend Alejandro. He is also a member of the Church. Look—Roberto,” he said looking at me and addressing me, “is not a member, but it’s as if he were.”

**Roberto**: “Ah, how wonderful! Then he must be as good as you.”

I blushed a little and answered: “Well, it’s not that big a deal,” laughing nervously.

## The Unexpected Moment

After a while of settling in and talking a bit, Roberto took the initiative in a way I never expected:

**Roberto**: “It’s time to read the scriptures,” he said naturally. “Alejandro, can you read in First Nephi chapter twelve? That’s where we are. Ah, but first we’re going to say a prayer. Will you do it?”

“All right,” I answered, still processing what was happening.

I said the prayer with a heart full of wonder, and then we began to read the Book of Mormon in that specific chapter.

## The Spiritual Experience

I could not believe what was happening. I looked at Tito completely astonished, and he nodded, smiling with a mixture of pride and tenderness. The Spirit that was felt that night in that moment literally cannot be described with human words.

After the reading, Roberto shared a few words of reflection that reached me straight to the soul. He said he was deeply grateful to be in that place, sick, because otherwise he would not have come to know the true Gospel, and he admitted that before he would not have accepted it because of his pride and circumstances.

Then he added: “Now Tito, you can say the closing prayer and give thanks that Alejandro came.”

I could not hold back the tears that night. I truly felt like the most blessed person in the whole world.

## The Farewell and the Commitment

The next day, when I had to leave, they had just told Tito that he had been discharged and that during the day he would return home after completing some routine paperwork that his family would handle.

Then Tito turned to Roberto with words I will never forget:

**Tito**: “Roberto, you already know that when they discharge us, whether from here or from this life, remember…”

Roberto interrupted him immediately:

**Roberto**: “Yes, I already know. I must seek out the missionaries so they can teach me. There are some here and on the other side too. Of that I am completely sure.”

## The Lesson of Transforming Gratitude

What profound words, that reached the deepest part of my soul. What immense conviction I learned that day from these two special men.

I had never heard a person be genuinely grateful for having fallen ill. But Roberto, as he reflected on the scriptures we had read, expressed his gratitude for having come to know what matters most for life: the Gospel of Jesus Christ.

He understood that sometimes God allows us to go through difficult trials not as punishment, but as opportunities for our hearts to open to eternal truths that we would otherwise reject.

Roberto had discovered that there are things more important than physical health: spiritual health and the eternal hope that comes through knowledge of the plan of salvation.

## The Treasure of the Soul

What indescribable joy I felt that day. I consider this experience one of the most precious treasures for my soul.

I learned that true conversion can happen in the most unexpected places and in the most difficult circumstances. That the Spirit of the Lord can touch hearts in hospital beds, where physical pain becomes spiritual openness.

And that sometimes, those who seem to be teaching are in reality the ones who learn the most. That night, Roberto taught me about genuine gratitude, unwavering faith, and the eternal perspective that can transform even the hardest trials into recognized blessings.
