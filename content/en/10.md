# 10 The School Snack

*"It is more blessed to give than to receive."* - [Acts 20:35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/20?lang=eng&id=p35#p35)

When I was in elementary school, I remember that always, or almost every day, I took pastries in a carefully wrapped package. Each morning my mother put in two or three fresh pastries for us.

My father worked as a pastry chef at a bakery in the city of Montevideo, and every day he brought home pastries they gave him at the end of his shift. The owner of the bakery had the generous habit of telling his employees: "Whatever is left is yours."

One day, during the first days of class, at school recess there was a group of classmates (there were about four or five of them) who were watching my snack very closely. I noticed that they had brought nothing to eat during recess. So I decided to divide my snack so that they too could enjoy a pastry.

That is how we began sharing the pastries in the best way we could, and each day I shared my pastries with Juan, Miguel, Carlitos, José, and Marcos. It was a beautiful routine that filled our recesses with joy.

The weekend came, and as I talked with my parents after the meetings at the chapel, I told them what was happening to me at recess. They listened very attentively to my story. At first I thought it had not mattered much to them, and I said to myself: "Well, I think I am doing good. They don't have anything for a snack."

The new week began. Monday passed with nothing new, and Tuesday arrived. In the morning, as we were getting ready for school, I saw several carefully prepared packages on the kitchen table. I noticed that my mother had written something on each package: a name.

When she was about to hand me my usual snack to put in my backpack, my mother took the first package and said: "This one you give to Juan." I put it carefully in my backpack.

"This other one you give to Miguel," she continued, handing me the second package.

"This other one you give to Carlitos," she went on with the third.

"This other one you give to José, and this last one to Marcos," she finished, completing the series of packages with each name on them.

When I got to school and recess time came, I took out my snack as always. Under the attentive eyes of my classmates, who had already grown used to our ritual of sharing, I said:

"Juan, I have something special for you," and I handed him one of the packages with his name written on it.

Then I did exactly the same with Miguel, Carlitos, José, and Marcos, giving each one his own package.

You cannot imagine the joy that lit up their faces when they realized that each of them had his own package with his name written on it. Their eyes shone in a way I will never forget. That day I felt I was eating the most delicious pastries in the whole world.

My mother's generosity had turned a simple act of sharing into something much more meaningful: each child felt special, remembered, and valued as himself.

How grateful I am to have had a mother so attentive and loving, who gave the best she knew how to give, as every good mother does. She had not only listened to my story; she had acted to multiply the good I was trying to do.

That day I learned that when we share the little we have, God multiplies our capacity to bless others. And that true joy does not come from what we keep for ourselves, but from the light we kindle in the eyes of those who receive our love.
