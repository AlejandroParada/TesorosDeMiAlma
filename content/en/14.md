# 14 Grandfather’s Horses

*“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”* — [John 3:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/3?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)

My grandfather was a policeman, a man of integrity who was assigned to patrol a new area of the city. During his service he came to a place where they asked him for a bribe, but he flatly refused to take it.

As a consequence of his honesty, and to keep him from reporting a superior’s corruption, they reassigned him as punishment to a police stable on the outskirts of the city. There he found himself among rebellious, unbroken horses that had not been well cared for.

One day my uncles went on an errand to fetch the milk bottles from the store, but along the way they started playing soccer in the street and ended up breaking every bottle. As punishment, they would have to “face their father” when he got home from work. It was the era of the slipper and the belt as methods of discipline, but this mischief was so serious that those usual punishments would not be enough.

Grandfather told them: “Tomorrow you are coming with me.” The punishment was imminent, but they did not know where he would take them or what he would do to them for their disobedience.

After a long trip out of the city, he took them to his workplace. The children were afraid, because they knew the horses were rebellious and that “anything could happen.” Their small minds imagined every kind of terrible consequence.

“You stay here and wait,” he told them. He went to the stable, loosened the reins, and opened the doors to the horses, but they did not come out at once. He called them one by one by name, and the horses began to come out gradually. As he called them, they came to the place where their ration of food was waiting.

While the animals ate peacefully, Grandfather began to brush them with care and tenderness. The children watched in astonishment as those supposedly rebellious horses grew gentle under their father’s loving treatment. They noticed that the animals bore scars and marks from earlier mistreatment, and they felt deeply identified with them when they recognized that their own “marks and hurts” also came from moments of rebellion.

With time and constant care, the horses became entirely fit for patrol, transformed from troubled animals into trustworthy companions.

That day Grandfather chose mercy rather than justice. His children learned an invaluable lesson about obedience, forgiveness, and the transformation that comes from love. Their behavior changed forever.

My father, when years later he was hospitalized for his heart condition, told me this “treasure” while I cared for him in the hospital. Of all the children, he was the youngest, and he learned that lesson in a very special way. His father, my grandfather, did not only win the hearts of the horses; he also won the hearts of his children for life.

Like our Heavenly Father, my grandfather showed that love and patience can transform even the most rebellious hearts.
