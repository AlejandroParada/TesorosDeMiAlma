# I Believe Christ Would Do the Same

> **"And now, as ye are desirous to come into the fold of God, and to be called his people, and are willing to bear one another's burdens... and are willing to mourn with those that mourn; yea, and comfort those that stand in need of comfort."** (Mosiah 18:8–9)

> **"By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another."** (John 13:35)

## A night at the hospital

On one occasion, a family member was admitted to the ICU. The hours were long, filled with uncertainty and waiting. Our hearts were heavy, and the hospital waiting room became a small refuge where fear, prayer, and the longing for comfort all blended together.

## Those who came to accompany us

Little by little people began to arrive: friends, family, and acquaintances. Every presence was a blessing. Every hug, every short word, every shared silence lightened a little the weight of that night.

## A stranger who radiated peace

Among them there was also a stranger. He was in the waiting room, calm, and from him radiated a love and comfort that could be clearly felt. He did not talk much; he was simply there.

I approached him and asked if he knew my family member. He said that he did, and that he had joined the Church only recently.

## Newly baptized, already ready to minister

He told me he had been baptized the week before. He knew my relative, and when he learned what had happened he came of his own goodwill, responding quickly to the news. He did not wait to be formally called or for someone to assign him a visit: he simply came.

I thanked that good man for being there in such a difficult moment.

## The answer that marked my soul

Simply and without pretension, he said: "I believe Jesus Christ would do the same."

Those few words lit up the entire scene. It was not an elaborate speech or a prepared lesson. It was the living testimony of a disciple newly born in the covenant, who had already understood the heart of the Gospel: to love, to accompany, and to minister as the Savior would.

## The ministry of being present

I learned that night that sometimes the most powerful ministry does not consist of great speeches or spectacular solutions. It consists of being present. It consists of sitting in a waiting room, sharing another's sorrow, and letting the love of Christ be felt through a faithful presence.

That brother, baptized only a few days earlier, taught me more about discipleship than many theoretical lessons. His example reminded me that the Gospel of Jesus Christ is lived when we do—even in small ways—what He would do.

## A treasure of comfort

When I remember that night, I do not only think of the anguish of the hospital, but of the light brought by a stranger who became a brother. His visit became a blessing to our family and a quiet testimony that Christ still sends His disciples—even new ones—to comfort those who stand in need of comfort.

## The trial overcome

In time, and by the mercy of the Lord, that family member overcame that hard trial. He recovered, moved forward, and was able to resume life normally with his family. That night of fear in the ICU waiting room was not the end of his story, but a chapter of faith, comfort, and hope that we remember today with gratitude.
