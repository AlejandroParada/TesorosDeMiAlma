# 11 The School Principal

*“I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out. As a shepherd seeketh out his flock in the day that he is among his sheep that are scattered; so will I seek out my sheep.”* — [Ezekiel 34:11-12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/34?lang=eng&id=p11-p12#p11)

At my school, when I was a boy, there was a principal who was deeply loved and respected by the whole community. But when I looked at him from my child’s point of view, he seemed to me a hard, rigid, and serious man. He was thin and tall, always wore a gray overcoat that made him look like a foreigner, and he wore the regulation gown at school.

Whenever I passed the principal’s office — whether to take some document or paper the teacher was sending to his office by way of a student, or because I had to go through that door to leave the school — I felt a mixture of respect and fear.

I looked on the principal’s office as that fearful place, because to me it was “the last place on earth a child would want to go.” In my mind as a fifth-grade student, that office stood for the final punishment for misbehaving at school.

But gradually my perspective changed in a surprising way.

## The discovery of his true character

When the door of his office opened now and then, I was able to watch Principal Shubert listening with deep attention to a worried teacher, to an anguished father, or to a staff member with some problem. I even remember a humble candy seller who had been assaulted in the street; the principal not only listened to him patiently, but even embraced him with compassion.

His presence radiated a special peace during school assemblies. I never heard him shout, not even to bring order among hundreds of restless children. Little by little, the principal’s office came to symbolize for me a place of love and self-forgetfulness, not of punishment and fear.

There came a time when people who no longer had any direct connection with the school — former students who had grown up and formed families of their own — came specifically to speak with him and hear his wise counsel. Among the children there circulated the reputation of how their parents had been listened to with genuine attention by this extraordinary principal.

## The rainy night that revealed everything

It was late at night, raining hard, and someone knocked at the door of my grandparents’ apartment, where we lived.

“Who could it be?” we asked one another, surprised by a visit at that hour.

When the door opened, I saw a thin, tall figure under a soaked gray overcoat. I recognized him at once: it was Principal Shubert! There was little light in the hallway, and he was accompanied by a very worried and distressed woman, both of them sheltering under a large black umbrella.

My mother, surprised but hospitable, said to him: “Principal, come in, please; don’t stay out in the rain. What brought you here?”

He answered with his characteristic gentleness: “Thank you very much. We are going from house to house, visiting all the fifth-grade students. We are looking for Carlitos Gómez. He has not come home since noon. This is his mother. Haven’t you seen him? Do you know which way he might have gone?”

I answered him honestly that I had not seen him, and that I had not paid attention to where he might have gone after class.

## The unwearying search

Carlitos had not returned home, and the police required that he be missing for twenty-four hours — an eternity for any mother — before they would officially begin to look for him. But Principal Shubert did not wait for the bureaucratic protocols.

I did not know at once what had happened to Carlitos, but later we learned that the principal and the boy’s mother finally found him at the home of a distant cousin of the family, where he had gone to play and had fallen asleep.

What I can affirm with complete certainty is that Principal Shubert heard a mother’s desperate heart and set himself in motion at once. They had begun the search early, surely walking for hours in the rain, visiting one by one the homes of his classmates, many of which, like ours, had no telephone in those days.

## The lesson of the Good Shepherd

Now I understood why Principal Shubert was so deeply loved. He forgot himself completely in order to serve others. He did not mind getting wet in the torrential rain, walking kilometers in the dark, or sacrificing his night’s rest.

I sincerely believe he exemplified the selfless love of the Savior, who said that the good shepherd knows his sheep and goes in search of the one that is lost. Principal Shubert was not only an educational administrator; he was a true shepherd of young souls.

This experience forever transformed my understanding of true leadership: it is not a matter of imposing authority from an office, but of serving with love from the heart.

I consider him an invaluable treasure to my soul, and a perfect example of how genuine love can change lives and entire communities.
