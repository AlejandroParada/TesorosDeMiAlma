11 The School Principal

"...I myself will search for my sheep and seek them out" (Ezekiel 34:11)

In my school when I was little, there was a principal who was very beloved and well-known. But when I looked at him, he seemed like a hard, rigid, and serious man. He was tall and thin with a gray overcoat—he seemed foreign—and wore the tunic, that gown in the school. Whenever I passed by the principal's office, whether to deliver some document or paper that the teacher sent to his office through a student, or because I had to pass through that door to leave school.

I looked at the Principal's office as that place with fear, because it was the "last place on Earth a child would want to go" since it was punishment for misbehaving at school (that was the way a fifth-grader thought). But gradually my perspective changed, when I saw when the door opened sometimes, I saw Principal Shubert listening very attentively to a teacher, a parent, or service staff, even a candy vendor who had been assaulted—he listened to him, even hugged him. His presence radiated peace during school assemblies. I never heard him shout, not even to maintain order. The Principal's office came to symbolize a place of love and selflessness. There came a time when people who had nothing to do with the school—former students who wanted to talk with him, hear his advice—it was a reputation that some children talked about, how their parents were listened to with attention.

It was late at night, raining heavily, and someone knocked on my grandparents' apartment door. Who could it be, we wondered. When I opened the door I could see a tall, thin figure in a gray overcoat, and I recognized him immediately—it was Principal Shubert! There was little light and he was with a very worried and distressed lady under a large umbrella. He told my mother, "Principal, come in, please, don't stay in the rain. What brought you here?"

He said, "Thank you very much. We are visiting house by house of all the fifth-grade students. We are looking for Carlitos Gomez—since noon he hasn't arrived home. Here is his mother. Haven't you seen him? Or do you know where he might have gone?" he asked me. I answered that I hadn't seen him, or paid attention to where he might have gone or anything.

Carlitos hadn't arrived home and the Police required that his absence be 24 hours (an eternity for a mother) before they would start looking for him. I didn't know what else happened with Carlitos, but later we learned that the principal and his mother found him at a family cousin's house. What I can affirm is that Principal Shubert listened to a mother and they set out to search for traces early, surely spending hours visiting many homes without telephones like ours in that era.

Now I understood why Principal Shubert was so beloved—he forgot about himself. I believe he exemplified the Savior's selfless love, and I consider him a treasure for my soul.
